All recordings were made by Python through NI-IMAQdx (decision 0021). Each plan ran in its own V6 `rig_session`,
and every motion went through V6 `motor_gate.py`. Raw data is in `G:\l2s_rig_data\2026-10-26_refbead\`, one folder
per plan.
- Tools: [tools/refbead_record.py](../../tools/refbead_record.py) and [tools/refbead_analyze.py](../../tools/refbead_analyze.py).
- Each folder holds `analysis.json` with the full numbers.
- 10 beads were picked from one snapshot (`snap/beads.json`). Beads 3 and 5 are not stuck (Brownian motion of

**Status: preliminary.** One sample, one session. The numbers are medians over the 8 fixed beads unless stated.

## 1. The pixel size is about 203 nm, not 84 nm (field plan)
ASI single-axis moves of 5, 10, 20 and 35 um were compared with the bead displacement on the image. Beads were
located by Croquette XY, and the global shift was found by phase correlation after removing the fixed background.

| move | X: nm / px | Y: nm / px |
|---|---|---|
| 5 um | 202.4 | 202.3 |
| 10 um | 205.3 | 204.7 |
| 20 um | 203.8 | 202.0 |
| 35 um | 204.6 | 200.5 |

- **The size is about 203 nm per binned pixel**, and X is about 2 % larger than Y at 35 um. The LabVIEW panel
  constant is 84.0 nm. 203 nm is what the optics predict: a 5 um sensor pixel, 2 x 2 binning and a 50x objective
  give 200 nm.
- **The camera is rotated about 2.3 deg against the stage.** An X move gives dy/dx = 0.040, and a Y move gives
  dx/dy = 0.040.
- **Sign:** +Y on the stage moves the image toward -y.
- **Return error:** every single-axis move pair came back within 0.3 px. After the whole 3 x 3 grid the field was
  off by (5.0, -10.9) px, about 1 / 2.2 um, which looks like stage hysteresis.
- **Open:** the ASI unit (10 000 per mm, read from `UM`) is taken as the truth. Every nm value written by LabVIEW
  for x / y uses 84 nm. Which constant the downstream MATLAB uses has to be checked before any number is changed
  (decision 0008: computation stays identical unless the owner decides).

## 2. Noise at rest (static plan, magnet at 0 mm, 60 s per condition)
- **No frame was lost** at 150 Hz or at 90 Hz. Both ran for 60 s on a fixed exposure of 1909 us.
All nm values here use the measured 203 nm / px.
- **Pure tracking noise is about 2.5-3 nm on both axes.** This is the bead-to-bead differential, std(a - b) / sqrt 2:
  2.4-3.1 nm at 150 Hz and 2.7 nm at 90 Hz. It is isotropic.
- **The common motion of the whole field is larger in y:** about 24 nm rms in y and about 7 nm in x within 60 s.
  Almost all of it is below 0.5 Hz (slow drift). There is also a line at 29.9 Hz.
  - So the y > x asymmetry that AnalyzeMT V5 found on its reference bead (sigma_y / sigma_x = 1.75) is motion of
    the sample. It is not camera or tracking noise.
  - Subtracting a reference removes it, but a single reference also adds its own ~3 nm.
- **Auto exposure:** the first 20 s after the session start (the PI reference move) are settling: 39 nm
  differential. After that, auto exposure gave 1.2-2.2 nm.
- Crops in this plan were 80 px (cross 60). All later plans use 160 px crops (cross 120, as in LabVIEW).

## 3. Z look-up table from a z-scan (zscan plan)
- **Scan:** focus from -5 to +5 um in 101 steps of 100 nm through the new V6 Z gate (`5d52080d`). Every step was
  read back, and the focus returned to its start (Z = 0).
- **LUT:** the mean radial profile per slice and bead was mirrored like the `.cal` stack. LOW 0, HIGH 25 and
  forget 20 were taken from the LabVIEW `.cal` of 2026-09-07.
- **Self-check, the same frames tracked back** (in-sample, so optimistic):
  - The tracked z follows the commanded z with a slope of 1.000 for every bead.
  - Per-frame z noise is 6-10 nm in the middle of the range and rises to 20-120 nm within 1 um of the ends.
- **Open:** comparing these LUTs with a LabVIEW-made `.cal` of the same beads (the phase 4 acceptance) needs a
  LabVIEW calibration.

### 3a. LUT step size, and pattern size vs z (owner 2026-10-27, offline from the same z-scan)
- **Method:** LUTs were built from every m-th slice of the 100 nm scan, giving 100, 200, 300 and 500 nm spacing.
  Each LUT uses the first half of each slice's frames. The second half of every slice's frames is the test, so
  slices that fall between LUT slices are out of sample.
- **Scope:** 8 fixed beads, over +-4 um, with the offset removed. The values are medians.

| LUT step | rms error (nm) | bias on LUT slices (nm) | bias between LUT slices (nm) | noise (nm) | slope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 nm | 14.1 | 8.9 | - | 7.3 | 1.000 |
| 200 nm | 17.5 | 11.7 | 14.5 | 7.5 | 1.000 |
| 300 nm | 20.2 | 11.5 | 19.6 | 7.1 | 1.001 |
| 500 nm | 24.6 | 14.5 | 23.6 | 7.4 | 0.998 |

- **Effect of a coarser step:** the per-frame noise stays the same, and the scale stays right. What grows is the
  interpolation bias between LUT slices: about +3 nm rms per extra 100 nm of step. LabVIEW's 100 nm (the `.cal` of
  2026-09-07: 60 slices x 100 nm) is the best spacing tested here.
- **A finer step could not be tested here** at first (see section 10: the gate now takes decimal Z, and 50 / 20 nm steps were measured). What it would buy can be estimated:
  - **The floor is not LUT noise.** At 100 nm, the bias on the LUT slices themselves stays at about 9 nm whether a
    slice averages 2, 5 or 15 frames (10.7 / 9.4 / 8.9 nm; rms 15.5 / 14.4 / 14.0 nm).
  - **Likely causes:** irregular real piezo steps, focus drift during the 1.5 min scan, or a bias of the
    5-neighbour phase fit.
  - **Expected gain of 50 nm:** extrapolating the trend (about 3 nm rms per 100 nm of step), roughly 12-13 nm rms
    against 14 nm. The scan time doubles, which adds focus drift (30-46 nm/min measured).
  - **Better levers than a finer step:** a faster scan or drift correction during the scan (a reference bead in z),
    and checking the piezo step itself.
- **Pattern size:** the radius of the first bright ring grows by **0.73-0.81 px per um of z** (about 150-165 nm per
  um at 203 nm / px), roughly linearly over +-4 um. For bead 0 it is 12.1 px at -4 um, 15.5 px at 0 and 17.9 px at
  +4 um. So one 100 nm step changes the ring radius by only about 0.075 px. The z information comes from the whole
  profile (the phase method), not from the ring size alone.
- **Not checked:** whether the piezo's real step equals the commanded 100 nm. The images cannot tell, because the
  LUT defines z. An independent length reference would be needed.

### 3b. Lower z noise at 50x by calibration parameters (owner 2026-10-27)
The owner moved to a lower-magnification objective and sees more noise. The question was which parameters of the
current method compensate for it.
- **Method:** offline on the recorded z-scans, with `tools/refbead_params.py`. The full table is in
  `docs/results/2026-10-27-refbead-params.json`.
- **Noise measure:** the per-frame z scatter inside a slice, taken as the difference of two fixed beads / sqrt 2.
  This removes the common stage and focus motion. It is the median over 28 bead pairs, from 8 beads, over +-4 um.
- **LUT and test frames:** the LUT is made from the first half of each slice's frames and tested on the second half.

| setting (LOW / HIGH / forget, cross 120) | z noise (nm) | slice error (nm) |
|---|---|---|
| **0 / 25 / 20 (the LabVIEW `.cal` of 2026-09-07)** | **7.25** | 8.4 |
| 0 / 30 / 10 | 4.97 | 7.7 |
| **0 / 40 / 10 (proposed)** | **4.64** | 7.7 |
| 0 / 50 / 10 | 4.59 | 7.7 |
| 0 / 40 / 0 | 6.00 | 7.8 |
| 0 / 15 / 10 | 13.3 | 9.3 |

- **The band-pass top (HIGH) and the forget radius are the two levers.** Raising HIGH from 25 to 40 and lowering
  forget from 20 to 10 px cuts the z noise by about a third (7.25 -> 4.64 nm). The accuracy also improves.
- **LOW** (0 / 2 / 4) matters little.
- **Above 40, and between forget 5 and 10, the result is flat.** Forget 0 is worse, because the bead's centre then
  enters the profile.
- **What forget does (checked in the port of `Tracking-prep I of r.vi`):** it removes the INNER radii. The
  profile from r = forget to r = cross / 2 - 1 enters the z fit. At 50x, with the bead in focus, the first dark
  minimum lies at 9-10 px, the first bright ring at 15-16 px and the second minimum at 20 px. Forget 20 therefore
  drops the first bright ring.
- **Size of the gain:** the z noise goes from 7.25 to 4.64 nm, -36 %. In variance, the tracking floor goes from
  53 to 22 nm^2, -60 %. That is what enters a force calibration from fluctuations, through sigma^2.
  - The two levers separately: forget 20 -> 15 at HIGH 25 gives 5.62 nm (-22 %). HIGH 25 -> 40 at forget 20 gives
    5.99 nm (-17 %).
- **Why the 50x needs this:** at 203 nm / px the ring pattern covers about 2.4 times fewer pixels than at 84 nm / px.
  The z information therefore sits at higher spatial frequencies of the radial profile, which a HIGH of 25 cuts off.
  The core and the first rings also lie closer to the centre, so a 20 px forget radius throws away useful rings.
- **Checks:**
  - Over the whole +-4.9 um, the noise is 7.25 -> 4.75 nm, and the edge error 28.9 -> 18.3 nm.
  - On the second sample (2026-10-27, one bead, a single-bead measure that includes the common motion): 6.50 ->
    5.18 nm.
- **Other parameters tested, with little or no effect on the noise:**
  - **Profile length (cross 80 / 100):** cross 80 with 0 / 39 / 10 gives 4.54 nm, the same within the scatter.
    Cross 120 can stay.
  - **Frames averaged per LUT slice (1 / 3 / 7 / 15):** no effect on the noise (7.1-7.3 nm). It only helps the
    accuracy (13.1 -> 8.4 nm), so keep >= 7.
  - **LUT step (20 / 40 / 60 / 100 nm, from the 20 nm scan, LUT from the way up, test on the way down):** noise
    6.0-6.5 nm for every step. The 130 nm slice error there is the piezo's up / down hysteresis, not the LUT.
- **How to use it:** LOW, HIGH and forget are stored per bead in every `.cal` (`bead_i32`). They are calibration
  settings, so changing them changes no computation of the program. Whether to set 0 / 40 / 10 for the 50x is the
  owner's call. The first check would be one LabVIEW calibration with these values and the same beads.
- **Not checked:** tethered beads (larger z motion, more defocus), and other samples.

## 4. Magnet position changes the apparent position of fixed beads (magnet plan)
- **Ramp:** 0 -> 39 mm -> 0 mm, holding 20 s at each step, about 9 min in total.
- **Drift:** the focus drifted by **+32 nm/min in z** during the ramp, and x by -5 nm/min.
- **Model:** the magnet effect below comes from a least-squares model `value = c + drift * t + effect(position)`,
  with the way up and the way down sharing one effect per position.

| position (mm) | 10 | 20 | 25 | 28 | 30 | 32 | 34 | 35 | 36 | 37 | 38 | 39 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dz (nm) | +39 | +17 | +44 | +93 | +126 | +146 | +148 | +108 | +133 | +95 | +122 | +145 |
| dx (nm) | -16 | -17 | -20 | -45 | -63 | -70 | -66 | -58 | -53 | -53 | -63 | -49 |
| dy (nm) | -17 | +12 | +44 | +50 | +41 | +27 | +81 | +95 | +79 | +51 | +53 | +64 |
| grey | +0.5 | -1.2 | -1.7 | -3.0 | -2.3 | -2.2 | -2.2 | -2.4 | -1.5 | -1.5 | -0.5 | -0.1 |

nm values use 203 nm / px. The residual rms of the model is 18 nm (z) and 11 nm (x, y).
- **Effect:** with the magnet at 28 mm or closer, non-magnetic fixed beads appear about **100-150 nm higher in z**,
  shifted about **60 nm in -x and 50-90 nm in +y**, and the image is about 2 % darker. The radial profile changes by
  about 0.3 of its modulation.
- **Cause not yet separated.** The LED fiber rides on the magnet's PI stage today, so the illumination moves with
  the magnet. Repeating this plan after the LED is mounted independently separates the two.
- **Design note:** the focus drift (32 nm/min) is as large as the effect. The next ramp should return to 0 mm
  between steps.

- **Open:** whether the stretch biases z is answered by the 3 x 3 z-scan (zscan_field). There, the same beads get a

## 8. A LUT is valid only at the field position where it was made (zscan_field plan)
- **Method:** the same z-scan (+-5 um, 100 nm) was run with the field moved to each of the 3 x 3 positions (stage
  +-35 um in x, +-28 um in y). The beads were found again at each position, and the same physical bead was matched
  to its centre position through the measured stage-to-image map (within 15 px).
- **Comparison:** every position's scan was tracked with the bead's own LUT made at that position, and with the same
  bead's LUT made at the centre. The deviation from the commanded z is measured over +-4 um with the offset removed.

| position (i, j) | beads | rms, own LUT (nm) | rms, centre LUT (nm) | slope with the centre LUT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (-1, -1) | 3 | 7-10 | 80-122 | 0.996-1.011 |
| (-1, 0) | 4 | 8-10 | 60-64 | 0.996-1.013 |
| (0, -1) | 4 | 9 | 123-166 | 1.030-1.054 |
| (0, 1) | 5 | 9-10 | 58-66 | 0.996-1.000 |
| (1, -1) | 4 | 7-8 | 56-120 | 0.995-1.043 |
| (1, 0) | 5 | 8-10 | 40-61 | 0.982-1.018 |
| (1, 1) | 2 | 9 | 55-72 | 0.998-1.017 |

(-1, 1) had no matched bead.
- **Result: moving a bead by about 30-45 um in the field changes its pattern enough to give 40-170 nm z
  nonlinearity and up to 5 % scale error** with a LUT made at the old position. With its own LUT the error stays at
  7-10 nm (in-sample).
- **What this means:** the field-position dependence of the pattern, including the outer stretch of section 5,
  matters for z. A bead must be calibrated where it is measured. That is what LabVIEW does today (one LUT per bead
  at its position), so the risk is a bead or stage that moves after the calibration. That is the same idea as U31
  (invalidate the LUT when the camera position changes), extended to the stage position.
- **Correction options, in order of effort:**
  1. Re-calibrate after any stage move larger than a few um, and flag a bead whose xy drifts.
  2. Keep a field map of LUTs and interpolate between positions.
  3. Fix the optics: the illumination alignment, while the LED is still on the magnet stage.
- **Caveats:**
  - The focus drift (about 30 nm/min) during each 1.5 min scan adds to both columns.
  - The own-LUT numbers are in-sample.
  - The bead numbers are those of the centre position's re-pick, not of `snap/beads.json`.

## 9. 30 min at rest (drift plan: magnet 0 mm, 150 Hz, 1909 us)
- **Lost frames: 76 of 269 824**, in three bursts at 5.4, 9.9 and 18.7 min (about 25 frames each). The short plans
  lost none. The cause is open: USB, the disk writes of this recorder, or another process. This is the first
  long-run frame-loss baseline for phase 6.
- **At about 20 min (about 01:06) every bead moved by 10-50 px** within a few minutes, in different directions. The
  sample was disturbed (cause unknown). The analysis below uses the first 19 min only.
- Some beads jumped by 10-20 px within the first minutes and were left out. Beads 4, 6, 7 and 8 stayed. XY is
  analysed at 30 Hz with cross 60, following the drift.
- **Common drift over 19 min:** x -45 nm and y -170 nm, about 3 / 6 nm per min.
  - Allan deviation of the common motion: at least 2.5 nm (x) at 0.1-0.3 s, rising to 27 nm (x) and 53 nm (y) at
    300 s.
  - **Differential (one bead minus another):** x stays at 1.3-3.4 nm from 0.03 s to 300 s, so referencing against
    a fixed bead removes the drift well. The y differential is 5-31 nm with this pair, larger than x. Open: it may
    come from the pair chosen.
- **Focus (z) drift: -46 nm/min, about -530 nm in 19 min.** Both beads drifted by the same amount (-528 / -537 nm),
  so it is a common focus drift. In the magnet ramp it was +32 nm/min. For z measurements, drift at this level
  argues for reference-bead subtraction in z, or a focus lock.

  found the following.
  - The ASI Tiger accepts `M Z=0.5` / `M Z=0.2` without error.
  - `W Z` reads back whole 0.1 um units only: `M Z=0.5` reads 1, `M Z=0.2` reads 0, and three `R Z=0.2` read 0,
    0, 0.
  - The gate therefore stays integer-only, so a 100 nm step is the finest scan today. A decimal-Z gate is kept
    unapplied as V6 `tools/bench/asiz2_decimal_z_not_applied.diff`.

- **The piezo moves in 50 nm and 20 nm steps (owner "1,2번 둘다 해봐", 07:17-07:44).**
  - **Gate:** the V6 gate now takes decimal Z (V6 `723471e6`). Read-back counts as reached when it is within
    0.5 unit. Every target is sent as an absolute `M Z=<decimal>` from the session's start Z.
  - **Repeated read-back (V6 `43ba3aaf`):** 50 `W Z` reads per target gave one value each. 0 and 0.3 read 0; 0.5
    and 0.7 read 1. The serial reply carries whole 0.1 um only.
  - **Controller display (the owner, at the rig):** the Tiger's own display shows the position in 10 nm units, and
    during the 20 nm scan it changed by 0.00002 mm per step. So the 0.1 um limit is the serial reply's
    resolution, not the controller's.
  - **Camera:** a sparse field, the best of a 5 x 5 search (+-600 um, 300 um pitch), had 2 beads. A 100 nm LUT was
    made there, then 50 nm steps over +-5 um and 20 nm steps over +-2 um, each up and back down. The bead with the
    clean pattern (LUT self-check 5-9 nm per frame) gives the following.

| scan | way | median measured step | 10-90 % of steps | steps < 5 nm | slope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 nm | up | 45.6 nm | 28-74 nm | 0 % | 0.999 |
| 50 nm | down | 46.8 nm | 24-71 nm | 1 % | 0.954 |
| 20 nm | up | 17.2 nm | 4-42 nm | 12 % | 0.909 |
| 20 nm | down | 18.7 nm | 5-39 nm | 10 % | 0.994 |

  - **Not a staircase.** A rounded 0.1 um staircase would give half the 50 nm steps at about 0 and half at about

  - **The step-to-step scatter** (tens of nm) is larger than the tracking error of one slice (1.2 nm). It comes from
    the piezo's settling within the 0.3 s wait, the focus drift during the 10 min scans, or real step
    irregularity; not separated. Over the whole scan the deviation from a straight line is 55-133 nm rms (drift or
    hysteresis).
  - **What it means for the LUT:** sub-100 nm steps are possible. Section 3a predicts only a small gain from a finer
    LUT, while the drift during a longer scan grows. A finer LUT is only worth it with drift correction during the
    scan.
